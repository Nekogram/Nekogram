# Nekogram Is Discontinued

## A Statement to Users

I offer my deepest apologies to everyone who trusted me and this project.

You installed Nekogram because you believed that open source allowed you to verify what was running on your devices. According to the published analysis of the release binaries, however, those binaries contained behavior absent from the public source code: collecting and transmitting Telegram account identifiers and phone numbers without users’ clear and informed consent.

This cannot be dismissed as a mistake, a misunderstanding, or a failure of communication. It violated the fundamental promise of an open-source project: that the published source code should correspond to the software users actually receive and run.

I betrayed real people - people who entrusted this application with their accounts, phone numbers, identities, and parts of their private lives.

Trust cannot be recovered through silence. It cannot be demanded back by pointing to years of development, the popularity of the project, or the existence of a public repository. Open source cannot serve as an alibi when distributed binaries contain behavior that is absent from the published source code.

I no longer have the right to ask users to simply trust me again.

None of these actions can undo what happened. Refusing to take them, however, would make the betrayal complete.

This project ends here.
